Life on the plains

Plants of the Comanchería

Buffalo fed the body, but the land fed everything else. Bows from bois d'arc, arrows from dogwood, meal from mesquite pods, plums for pemmican, and medicine from the prairie. Most of this knowledge was carried by women.

About these usesThe uses on this page were recorded from Comanche elders in the 1930s by Gustav Carlson and Volney Jones, and are summarized in later National Park Service research. They are history, not medical advice. Some plants with ceremonial or private uses are deliberately left out of this guide.
  • Gustav G. Carlson and Volney H. Jones, "Some Notes on Uses of Plants by the Comanche Indians," Papers of the Michigan Academy of Science, Arts and Letters 25 (1940)
  • National Park Service ethnobotany report, chapter 8 (plant uses with sources)
  • Daniel E. Moerman, Native American Ethnobotany (Timber Press, 1998)
