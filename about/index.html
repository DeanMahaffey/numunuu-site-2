What this is

numunuu.com is an independent site about Comanche history, language and life, made by a citizen of the Comanche Nation. It brings together exhibits, a map, a timeline, a game and an online dictionary, so that more of the Comanche story is easy to find in one place.

It is a personal project. It is not run by, affiliated with, or endorsed by the Comanche Nation, the Comanche National Museum and Cultural Center, or any language committee. Nothing here speaks for the Nation.

The history on these pages comes from published books, museum records, government documents and news reporting. Every page lists its sources at the bottom, and all of them are gathered below.

How we work

A few rules every page follows.

Sources on every page

Each page ends with what it was built from. Books and primary records come first. Wikipedia is a starting point, not the last word, and where it's cited, it sits next to other sources.

Comanche words as recorded

We only use spellings found in published sources. We don't make up spellings or "fix" old ones. Where a word hasn't been checked, we leave it out rather than guess.

Dates as the record gives them

Many dates come from Spanish, Texan and American records, and some are disputed. When sources disagree, we give the range and say so.

Names as they were written

On the treaties page, the signers' names are copied exactly as the clerks wrote them, even when the spelling is clumsy. Changing them would erase the record of who was in the room.

Some things are left out

Ceremonial and private knowledge doesn't belong on a public website. The plant guide skips plants with ceremonial uses, and nothing here teaches ceremony.

Outside eyes, named as such

Much of the old record was written or painted by outsiders, like George Catlin in 1834. We show it because it's what survives, and we say whose eyes it came through.

Painted scenes are made by code

The landscapes in the page banners and the game are drawn by the site's own painter. They are pictures, not historical images, and they show the country in general terms.

History, not advice

Plant uses are recorded history, not medical advice. Hours and directions on the visit page are checked against public listings, so confirm with the museum before a long drive.

All sources

Every source used across the site, grouped by kind. Pick a page to see only what it was built from, or use the tags to jump to the page that uses each one.

Images

Photographs and paintings come from museums and archives that make them free to use, either in the public domain or under an open license. Each image is credited where it appears, with its maker, date, collection and license.

Museum collections

Smithsonian American Art Museum, National Portrait Gallery, National Museum of the American Indian, Brooklyn Museum, Art Institute of Chicago, The Met, and others. We keep our own copies of their open-access images on this site, and the extra-large zoom versions load from the museums when you open a picture.

Archives

U.S. National Archives, U.S. Army, Missouri Historical Society, and The Portal to Texas History at the University of North Texas.

Wikimedia Commons

Some photos come through Wikimedia Commons, under the license shown on each one. Photos under CC BY-SA name their photographer.

Cite a page

For a school paper or an article. Pick a page and a style, then copy. Better still, cite the original sources the page lists, too.

Privacy

No ads, no accounts, and no tracking cookies.

What stays on your device

Follow the Herd saves your game, and the ancestors guide saves your checklist, in your own browser so you can pick up where you left off. None of it leaves your device, and clearing your browser data erases it.

What other sites see

Fonts load from Google Fonts, and a few large images load from museum and Wikimedia servers. Like any website, those servers can see your IP address when your browser asks for a file.

The dictionary

The dictionary at dictionary.numunuu.com is a separate app. Anything you send through a form here, such as a name for the honor wall, is used only for that purpose.

Send a correction

If you see a mistake, a missing name, a bad spelling or a date that's off, please say so. Corrections from Comanche families, elders and knowledge-keepers are especially welcome, and they come first.
