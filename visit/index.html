Visit the museum

The Comanche National Museum and Cultural Center is the Comanche Nation's own museum, opened in 2007 in Lawton. Here is everything you need to plan a visit, and a few places nearby to make a day of it.

Inside

What you'll find

The museum tells Comanche history from the Comanche point of view, from the southern plains to today. Exhibits change, so this is a guide, not a promise.

Comanche history

Galleries on Comanche life on the southern plains, horses and leadership, and the hard years of reservation and allotment, with photographs, clothing, beadwork, and treaty documents.

Read Turning Points first →
The Code Talkers

A section on the Comanche men who carried their language into World War II, with a focus on Charles Chibitty, the last surviving Comanche code talker.

Meet the seventeen →
Comanche artists

Fine art by Comanche artists, from traditional to contemporary, with a gallery for changing shows of painting, sculpture, and mixed media.

See the 1834 paintings →
Collections and archives

The Egli and Chibitty Collections, items from the Fort Sill Indian School and Comanche military service, plus a library and photo archives for research.

Gift shop

Books, blankets, beadwork supplies, and recordings. A good place to find work by Comanche makers.

For kids

Hands-on pieces like a lodge to step inside and a buffalo hide to touch. Lectures, workshops, and community events run through the year.

Make it a day

Around Lawton

Comanche history doesn't stop at the museum door. These places are all within about half an hour.

1

Fort Sill National Historic Landmark and Museum

The old post where Comanche and Kiowa families came in during the 1870s. Quanah Parker is buried at Chief's Knoll in the post cemetery, and Geronimo's grave is on the post too.

Tue–Sat, 9 am – 5 pmFree580-442-5123

Fort Sill is an active Army post. Stop at the Visitor Control Center for a background check before you enter, and bring photo ID.

2

Wichita Mountains Wildlife Refuge

Granite mountains, open prairie, and free-roaming bison, longhorn, and elk. The dragoons of 1834 marched through these mountains after leaving the Comanche village Catlin painted.

Open sunrise to sunsetVisitor center daily, 9 am – 5 pmFree

From I-44, take Highway 49 west (exit 45). Give bison plenty of room; they are wild and fast.

3

Museum of the Great Plains

Right down Ferris Avenue in Elmer Thomas Park.

The main building closed after August 15, 2026 for repairs, with reopening expected in early 2028. Its outdoor sites stay open weekdays, 10 am to 5 pm, free.

4

Comanche Nation Fair

If you can time a trip for it, come in the fall. The Comanche Nation's biggest gathering has a powwow, a parade, an art show, handgame and other tournaments, and honors Comanche veterans every day. Free, at the Comanche Nation Complex north of Lawton.

Each fall584 NW Bingo Rd
