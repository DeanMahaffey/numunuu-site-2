    About the Project

    Ink & Sovereignty

    Treaties are often taught as moments of surrender. Read closely, they show something else: a people negotiating band by band, as autonomous political bodies, with empires and republics that frequently could not or would not keep their side of the paper.

    This exhibit puts each document on the table and asks three questions:

    • Who was in the room? Comanche bands were sovereign. A chief's mark bound his own people, not the whole Nation.
    • What was promised? Key articles in plain English, with links to the full transcript.
    • What happened? A forensic audit of promise against outcome.

    On names and language. Treaty clerks wrote Comanche names by ear in English or Spanish spelling. Those recorded forms are kept exactly. The Comanche orthography field (Nʉmʉ Tekwapʉ) stays marked pending until a spelling has been checked against the Comanche Language and Cultural Preservation Committee's dictionary. No pronunciation is invented: where no recording of a fluent speaker exists, the play button sounds a neutral placeholder tone, one beat per recorded syllable.

    Data basis. Each treaty is marked Transcript when names and terms were taken from the OSU transcript, or Secondary when they come from published histories and should be checked against the primary record.
