Beads, paint, and fringe

Comanche makers, most of them women, dressed their families in painted hide, long twisted fringe, and beadwork used with a sure and sparing hand. Here is what to look for, and some fine pieces from public collections.

Look forHow to know Southern Plains work

Less is more

On the northern plains, makers often covered whole garments in quills and beads. Southern Plains makers, the Comanche and Kiowa among them, took the opposite road. Hides were dyed or painted in warm, soft colors. Fringe was long, thin, and twisted. Beads were used as trim: along an edge, down a seam, across the toe of a moccasin.

Northern Plains
  • Large fields of beadwork or quillwork
  • Often white, naturally tanned hide
  • Bold, all-over designs
Southern Plains
  • Beadwork as trim and accent
  • Hide painted or stained yellow, green, red
  • Long, thin, twisted fringe
  • German silver buttons

Reading these objects

Every piece here was made by a person whose name was almost never written down.

Makers without names

Collectors recorded the tribe, sometimes, and rarely the maker. Some labels still say "Kiowa or Comanche" because the two peoples lived side by side and their work is close.

Far from home

These pieces now sit in Toronto, Brooklyn, Chicago, and elsewhere. Some were sold in hard times, some were collected in ways we will never know.

Still being made

Comanche beadworkers carry these traditions today. See their work in person at the Comanche National Museum and the art show at the Comanche Nation Fair.
